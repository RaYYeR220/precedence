How Solana leaders ordered our transactions

We sent small batches of probe transactions to known leaders, with fees and send times we chose in advance, then read back where each one landed in the block. A rule is claimed only when it beats random ordering in a permutation test; its strictness is the share of probe pairs it puts in the observed order.

Rule by client family

The rule card names what decides the order in each condition, claiming only what beats random. The best single rule is the one model that fits all batches at once; its strictness is the share of pairs it orders correctly, 95% interval over batches, on a scale from 50% (coin flip) to 100%. Does a later, higher fee still win?

Share of probe pairs where the higher-priority probe came first, by how much later it was sent. A local scheduler keeps the fee winning across the gap; a batch auction stops honouring it once the gap crosses a batch boundary.

Equal fees: oldest or newest first

Tie batches: eight probes with the same fee, sent 0.5 to 5 ms apart. Share of pairs where the earlier-sent probe came first.

Did the probes land

By the family of the leader we targeted. The hairline marks how many landed in another leader's block.

Naming the orderer from our probes alone

A classifier sees only our probes' order, timing and landing, and names the component that ordered the block. Each validator is scored by a model trained without it. The baseline sees every transaction of the same blocks instead.
